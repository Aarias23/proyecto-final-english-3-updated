FIVE TEAMS · ONE INTEGRATED CHALLENGE

Make a plan.
Make your case.

Develop a practical group project connected to everyday life. Submit a written report in English and present your project during the synchronous class meeting. Explain what happens now, what happened before, what you need and what you will do next. Compare options and defend your choice in English.

5 projects3 students per team8–10 min + 2 min questions100 points
Teacher guidance. This guide proposes the final-project format and rubric. All five groups integrate the same content through different situations. Education and American Culture are excluded from the project and assessment. Complete Probability and Future and Comparative and Opinions after Units 4 and 5 are taught. Deadline and submission method: to be announced by the teacher. The written report and presentation must be in English.

01 / THE SHARED BRIEF

A proposal, not a grammar lecture.

Use English to solve your group’s challenge. Your project must include present and past actions, countable and uncountable nouns, partitives, quantifiers, probability, future plans, comparisons and opinions. Do not assign one course unit to each group: every team integrates all these areas.

Written group report

Submit 3–4 pages of content in English, plus a cover page and a list of sources. Include these sections:

  1. Introduction: your idea, why you chose it and your goal.
  2. Present situation: relevant habits, needs or characteristics.
  3. Past experience: a real or clearly labeled fictional experience that informs your decisions.
  4. Two alternatives: a comparison table, your choice and reasons.
  5. Resources: a table of at least 8 items with units, quantities, what you have and what you need. Include at least 3 countable and 3 uncountable noun meanings.
  6. Future plan: steps, responsibilities, expected results, possible difficulties and a backup plan.
  7. Group conclusion: explain why your proposal is suitable and what you learned.

Include both tables within the 3–4 content pages. Use readable formatting, connected paragraphs and your own words. Credit borrowed information and images.

Synchronous presentation

Prepare 5–7 slides and present your project for 8–10 minutes during the synchronous class meeting. Allow about 2 additional minutes for questions.

Explain the idea, past experience, alternatives, quantities, future plan and conclusions. All three members must speak, justify decisions and answer questions. Share speaking time fairly and use short notes.

Each member should be ready to explain the whole project. This is a presentation of your project; a role-play or dramatization is not required.

You may use clearly labeled fictional project data. You do not need to spend money, travel, open a business or hold an event. For the seven-day challenge, complete a simple activity if possible, or present a detailed plan and clearly distinguish expected results from observed results.

The same targets for every team.

Our Ideal Trip

Plan a trip with friends.

  • YOHANNA YERENIA ACEVEDO CAMPOS
  • ADAURIS ROSANA ALVAREZ VERAS
  • RAINER CUPETE MODESTO

Your challenge

Compare two destinations and choose the one that best fits your group’s interests and resources.

  1. Describe your usual travel habits, preferences and needs. Recount a past real or fictional trip and what you learned from it.
  2. Compare two destinations, accommodation options and activities using cost, time and convenience. Choose one destination and justify your decision.
  3. Prepare an itinerary and a packing and supplies table. Explain quantities of food, drinks and personal supplies, assign tasks, predict possible problems and offer a backup plan.

Suggested visuals: A destination comparison chart, an itinerary and a packing list.

Presentation focus: Explain your project and decisions to your classmates. All members participate and answer questions; no dramatization is required.

Guiding question: Which destination is the best choice for our group, and why?

Member 1: Travel habits and past experience.

Member 2: Destination comparison, accommodation and supplies.

Member 3: Itinerary, responsibilities, predictions and conclusion.

  • ASHLEY GUZMAN CASTILLO
  • IVANIA GUADALUPE GUZMAN SORIANO

Your challenge

Choose a manageable idea such as baked goods, juices or gift packages. Compare two products or service options and recommend one.

GROUP 5

Our Seven-Day Challenge

Design a group challenge connected to everyday habits.

  • DANYELIS REYES
  • JOLENNY REYES MEJIA
  • ENEROLISA RODRÍGUEZ CORDERO

Your challenge

Choose cooking at home, reducing waste, organizing time or another simple daily activity. Complete it if practical, or submit a clearly labeled detailed plan.

  1. Describe starting habits and the goal. Recount a related past experience. If you complete the challenge, also report actions you actually carried out.
  2. Compare two approaches and choose one with reasons. Prepare a resource and quantities table suited to your activity, including physical materials or supplies.
  3. Create a seven-day schedule and divide responsibilities. If completed, compare starting and final observations. If planned only, compare the two approaches and describe expected results as predictions, not past facts. Explain difficulties and next steps.

Suggested visuals: A seven-day log or planning calendar, an approach comparison and a resources table. Label observed and expected results separately.

Presentation focus: Explain your project and decisions to your classmates. All members participate and answer questions; no dramatization is required.

Guiding question: What can we change in seven days, and how will we know whether it helps?

Member 1: Starting habits, goal and real or fictional past experience.

Member 2: Approach comparison, resources and recorded or expected results.

Member 3: Seven-day schedule, predictions, next steps and conclusion.

The roles above follow the order of the member list and help you organize preparation. You may exchange roles. All three members help write, review and rehearse the whole project; all three present and answer questions.

04 / BUILD IT IN STAGES

From idea to presentation.

1 / Outline

Agree on your project, goal and two options. Draft the present and past context.

2 / Resources

Build the inventory. Check noun meanings, partitive units and quantifiers.

3 / Complete Units 4–5

After the lessons, add predictions, plans, comparisons and supported opinions.

4 / Rehearse

Check your report, time the presentation and practice answering questions without reading.

05 / ASSESSMENT

100 points. Clear evidence.

Team product: 70 points. Individual performance: 30 points. Each student receives the team score plus their own individual score. A strong presentation communicates meaning; a native accent is not required.

Proposed final-project rubric
CriterionPointsFull-credit evidence
Integrated language · team25Five areas, 5 points each: present simple; past simple; nouns/partitives/quantifiers; probability/future; comparisons/opinions. Required examples communicate the intended meaning accurately.
Proposal & reasoning · team20Clear goal, coherent present/past context, meaningful option comparison, justified recommendation and workable backup plan.
Resources & quantities · team15At least 8 items; quantities match the stated scenario; have/need distinctions and units are clear; shortages or excess are explained.
Deliverables & organization · team10The 3–4-page report, comparison and resource tables, and 5–7 slides are complete, readable and consistent. Sources and fictional data are identified. Presentation follows the format and time.
Clarity & language · individual15Student explains ideas understandably, uses more than one content area and speaks from brief notes with generally accurate language.
Interaction & answers · individual10Student explains and justifies project decisions, responds to classmates and answers at least one follow-up question.
Preparation & contribution · individual5Student explains their contribution, knows the shared proposal and takes a balanced speaking role.
How partial credit works · teacher guide

For each criterion, use these anchors: 100% = complete, clear and accurate evidence; 75% = mostly complete with minor errors or gaps; 50% = partial evidence with frequent errors or weak explanation; 25% = minimal evidence with substantial support needed; 0% = no evidence. For integrated language, apply these anchors separately to each 5-point area, then add the results. Round the final score to the nearest whole point.

Assess grammar under language criteria; assess resource reasoning under resources. An isolated language error should not automatically remove points from every criterion. During questions, ask each student to explain one choice, quantity or prediction from the shared proposal.
